# Capital allocation across strategy sleeves and sleeve-level risk budgeting

**Bottom line**
1. A 70/30 *capital* split is not a 70/30 *risk* split: if the intraday sleeve runs ~3× the swing sleeve's volatility, 30% of capital carries ~39/61 of the portfolio's variance. A two-sleeve equal-risk-contribution (ERC) target is exactly inverse-vol and correlation-independent, i.e. **~75/25**, not 70/30.
2. Never let each sleeve Kelly-size independently: two full-Kelly sleeves on correlated risk can put the house at ≥2× Kelly, where continuous-time excess growth collapses to ~zero (Thorp/Stutzer).
3. The sleeves *look* uncorrelated in calm markets and correlate in stress (Longin–Solnik: correlation rises in bear markets; Ang–Chen: downside correlation ~11.6% above normal-implied), so the house gate must size on a **stress correlation (~0.7)**, not a calm one (~0.1).
4. Costs decide the apples-to-apples comparison: at 100× annual turnover and ~10–11 bps/side, the intraday sleeve burns ~**20% of traded capital per year** before tax; a <1×-turnover swing sleeve burns <10 bps.

## Findings

1. **ERC is the formal risk parity.** Maillard–Roncalli–Teiletche define the portfolio where each asset's *total risk contribution* is equal; ERC always exists and is unique under their assumptions, lies between minimum-variance and equal-weight, and with uncorrelated assets reduces to inverse-volatility weighting. [1]
2. **Two-sleeve ERC = inverse-vol, correlation-free.** Setting RC_A = RC_B for two assets, w_A(w_Aσ_A² + w_Bσ_AB) = w_B(w_Bσ_B² + w_Aσ_AB). The cross terms cancel, leaving w_Aσ_A = w_Bσ_B, so w ∝ 1/σ. This is an exact algebraic result, independent of ρ [derive; consistent with 1].
3. **HRP** (Lopez de Prado 2016) replaces covariance inversion with hierarchical clustering + quasi-diagonalisation + recursive bisection; Monte Carlo showed lower out-of-sample variance than Markowitz's critical-line algorithm and lower risk than classical risk parity, and it survives ill-conditioned/singular covariance matrices. [2] A 2025 theoretical follow-up (Antonov–Lipton–Lopez de Prado) shows the *allocation noise* from an estimated covariance matrix is lower under HRP than Markowitz, i.e. HRP is provably more robust. [3]
4. **Risk budgeting arithmetic.** For a positively homogeneous risk measure, the Euler allocation RC_i = w_i·∂R/∂w_i sums exactly to portfolio risk R; the marginal contribution ∂R/∂w_i is the sensitivity, the component contribution is the budget. CVaR is coherent and positively homogeneous, so it admits a full Euler decomposition — this is what makes "risk budget as % of equity in CVaR terms" well-posed. [4]
5. **Worked example (70/30 vs ERC).** $100,000 equity; Swing $70,000 at 10% annualised vol; Intraday $30,000 at 30% vol. *Calm, ρ=0.10:* portfolio σ = 11.94%; risk contributions 4.63%/7.31% → **swing 38.8%, intraday 61.2%**. *Stress, ρ=0.70:* portfolio σ = 14.77% (+24% vs calm), contributions 6.30%/8.47% → **swing 42.7%, intraday 57.3%**; diversification benefit (1 − weighted-avg-standalone-σ / σ) falls from 25.4% to 7.7%. ERC target = 75/25. So the owner's 70/30 gives the *higher-turnover, higher-vol* sleeve more risk than the value sleeve. [derive; vol/corr inputs illustrative]
6. **Volatility targeting is evidence-backed but not universal.** Moreira–Muir: scaling down when volatility is high and up when low "produces large alphas, substantially increases factor Sharpe ratios"; the volatility-managed index beats the passive strategy in ~80% of cases; effect is strong for equities/factors, negligible for bonds, FX, commodities. [6] Harvey et al. (2018): US equity Sharpe rises 0.40 → 0.48–0.51 when vol-scaled, and vol targeting reduces max drawdown for balanced and risk-parity portfolios; Sharpe impact for bonds/FX/commodities is negligible. [7]
7. **Kelly's good and bad properties.** Main advantage: maximises the limiting exponential growth rate of wealth. Main disadvantage: "its suggested wagers may be very large… the Kelly criterion can be very risky in the short term." Fractional Kelly is simply a blend of the Kelly portfolio with cash; half-Kelly corresponds to negative-power utility with γ = 1, quarter-Kelly γ = 3 (lognormal case). [8]
8. **The 2×Kelly blow-up.** In continuous time, betting **exactly double** the Kelly amount makes the growth rate equal the risk-free rate (Thorp 1997; Stutzer 1998; Janacek 1998, restated in 8). This is the mathematical reason not to let two sleeves each size at full Kelly on overlapping risk: the book can reach 2× Kelly, where excess growth → 0 and risk is maximal.
9. **Simultaneous/multivariate Kelly must be joint.** With several bets active at once the correct objective maximises expected log growth over the *joint* allocation subject to a bankroll constraint; sizing each bet as if it were alone over-allocates. Estimation error is the dominant hazard: Chopra–Ziemba report certainty-equivalent losses from errors in *means* vs variances vs covariances of roughly **20 : 2 : 1**, and portfolio turnover is larger for mean errors than variance/covariance errors. [8]
10. **Kelly can still ruin a path.** 700 wagers each with a 14% edge (worst 19% win probability) can turn $1,000 into $18; even at full Kelly there is a 16.6% chance of ending ≥$100,000. Half-Kelly caps the bad tail at ~$145 and lowers growth. [8] → practical caps are mandatory.
11. **Correlation is regime-dependent, not stable.** Longin–Solnik use extreme-value theory and reject multivariate normality in the **negative tail but not the positive tail**; "correlation is not related to market volatility per se but to the market trend. Correlation increases in bear markets, but not in bull markets." [9] Ang–Chen find US equity portfolio correlations with the market are "much greater for downside moves, especially for extreme downside moves"; their asymmetry measure is ~11.6% above the bivariate-normal benchmark, strongest for small-cap, value and past-loser stocks. [10]
12. **Linear correlation hides dependence structure.** Embrechts–McNeil–Straumann show two distributions with identical Gamma marginals and identical correlation = 0.7 can differ qualitatively (one has extreme losses arriving together); linear correlation is not invariant under increasing transforms, and zero correlation implies independence only in the elliptical/normal world. Copulas (Gaussian vs Gumbel vs Student-t) are the right language for tail dependence. [11]
13. **"Diversification fails when you need it most" — the claim is directional, not absolute.** It is widely repeated (finance trade press) and is directionally supported by [9][10][11], but Campbell–Forbes–Koedijk–Kofman caution that part of the observed "correlation meltdown" can be an artifact of assuming normal returns; under a bivariate Student-t model they find weaker evidence of a genuine conditional-correlation increase. [12]
14. **Drawdown-aware throttling.** CPPI sizes risky exposure as *multiplier × cushion*, cushion = portfolio value − floor (Black–Jones). [13] Grossman–Zhou give the optimal continuous-time strategy for *controlling drawdown*. [14] A de-risking ladder is the discrete operational form: step the multiplier (or ratchet the floor) down as drawdown crosses thresholds, e.g. m = 4 → 3 → 2 → 1/0 as drawdown moves through 0–5%, 5–10%, 10–15%, ≥15%. [13][derive]
15. **Drawdown *duration* matters, not just depth.** The Ulcer Index (Martin, 1987) is the root-mean-square of percentage drawdowns from prior highs, so it rises with both depth and time-under-water; replacing SD with UI in the Sharpe numerator gives the Ulcer Performance Index / Martin ratio. [15]
16. **Transaction costs are the swing factor.** Frazzini–Israel–Moskowitz report average market impact **9.97 bps** and implementation shortfall **11.02 bps** (medians 7.36 / 9.42; value-weighted 17.06 / 18.34 bps). SMB realised an annual trading cost of **1.47%** at **53% monthly turnover**; an optimised design cut total cost from **88 bps/yr to 13 bps/yr**. [16]
17. **Turnover is a hard capacity filter.** Novy-Marx–Velikov: most anomalies with one-sided **monthly turnover below 50%** still generate statistically significant *net* spreads after costs; **few of those above 50% do**; costs reduce both profitability and statistical significance. [17]
18. **Broker mechanics for a shared pool.** Alpaca: margin/short requires ≥$2,000 equity; Reg T initial margin 50% (marginable) / 100% (non-marginable); **2× overnight buying power**, up to **4× intraday**; overnight maintenance 30% for stocks >$6, 50% for $2.50–$6, 100% <$2.50; concentration rule raises maintenance to 50% when one security is ≥70% of equity and margin balance ≥$100k; margin interest **6.25% non-elite / 4.75% elite** on the end-of-day debit, charged on a 360-day basis. [18]
19. **The PDT regime is gone.** FINRA Rule 4210 was overhauled (effective ~2026-06-04): the $25,000 minimum and "4 trades in 5 days" test are replaced by an **Intraday Margin Level (IML)** / **Intraday Margin Deficit (IMD)** framework. An IMD is **not** triggered by mark-to-market losses — only by customer-initiated IML-reducing transactions; it must be satisfied "as promptly as possible"; failure within 5 business days triggers a capital charge, and repeated failures a 90-day restriction on new debits/shorts, with de-minimis exceptions (<$1,000 or <5% of equity). The doc cites evidence that **intraday risk levels can be 50% higher than daily-close data implies**. [19]
20. **Operational separation is supported natively.** Alpaca **OmniSub** is one legal omnibus account with internal "sub-accounts" that are *not* brokerage accounts; trading originates only from sub-accounts; a post-trade net-settlement model aggregates and nets at end of day. [20] The Alpaca **Rebalancing API** implements exactly the sleeve-rebalancing primitives: **drift_band** conditions (absolute % and relative %), **calendar** conditions (annual/quarterly/monthly/weekly), and a **cooldown_days** period; weights include an explicit cash weight. [21]
21. **Multiple-testing discipline is required.** Bailey–Lopez de Prado: with enough trials almost any false strategy can be made to show any Sharpe ratio; the deflated Sharpe corrects for selection, backtest overfitting and non-normality. [25] This is the brake on "chase the sleeve that won last quarter".
22. **Allocation as a bandit problem works and is published.** Thompson sampling has been applied to portfolio choice by treating candidate strategies as arms and sampling from their posteriors each rebalance [27]; "Portfolio Blending via Thompson Sampling" (IJCAI 2016) learns *blending coefficients* over basis portfolios with Bayesian updates. [28]
23. **Chasing destroys returns.** Barber–Odean: the most active traders in a discount-brokerage sample earned ~**11.4%/yr** versus ~**17.9%** for the market and ~16.4% for the average household; the gap is largely transaction costs and poor trade timing. [29] This is empirical support for slow, banded reallocation with an explicit edge hurdle rather than frequent tilting.
24. **MCP/tooling surface exists.** Alpaca ships an official MCP server (FastMCP + OpenAPI, 65 tools; `ALPACA_TOOLSETS` env var enables read-only scoping such as `account,stock-data` only; `ALPACA_PAPER_TRADE` defaults true), which is the natural isolation boundary between the research layer and the execution layer. [30]

### Sleeve scorecard — metrics, definitions, and indoor caveats

| Metric | Definition | Why it misleads intraday | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sharpe | (R − R_f) / σ(returns), annualised | Penalises upside vol; assumes iid daily returns; 175-day sample → SE ~0.6 | [22][25] |
| Sortino | (R − T) / TDD, TDD = RMS of shortfalls below target T | Needs a target; unstable with few observations | [23] |
| Calmar | CAGR / \|max drawdown\| (commonly 36-month) | 3-yr window unusable on 8.5 months | [24] |
| MAR | CAGR since inception / \|max drawdown since inception\| | Same as above; single-path dependent | [24] |
| Profit factor | gross profit / gross loss | Sensitive to one outlier trade; not risk-adjusted | [26, practitioner] |
| Expectancy | (win% × avg win) − (loss% × avg loss) | Needs ≥100 trades to be meaningful | [26, practitioner] |
| Payoff ratio | avg win / avg loss | Says nothing about frequency | [26, practitioner] |
| Turnover | traded notional / avg capital, annualised | Must be paired with cost, else silently lethal | [16][17] |
| Capacity | notional at which costs erase edge | Intraday capacity is far lower than swing | [16][17] |
| Exposure / utilisation | time-in-market × avg deployed capital / allocation | Low utilisation = dead capital; repo it, don't waste it | [derive] |
| CVaR (1-day 97.5%) | E[loss \| loss ≥ VaR_97.5] | Needs ≥250 daily obs for a stable tail; intraday needs intraday sampling | [4] |
| Ulcer Index | sqrt(mean of squared % drawdowns) | Captures depth+duration — the owner's stated preference | [15] |
| Tail ratio | \|P95 return\| / \|P5 return\| | Small-sample noisy; define the percentile window explicitly | [derive; practitioner lead] |
| Marginal contribution to risk | RC_i = w_i·∂R/∂w_i (Euler) | Must be recomputed under stress ρ, not calm ρ | [4] |
| Deflated Sharpe | PSR adjusted for #trials, skew, kurtosis | Mandatory when selecting among sleeve variants | [25] |

## Contested or unproven

- **"Diversification meltdown" is not settled.** Campbell–Forbes–Koedijk–Kofman argue part of the apparent conditional-correlation rise is an artifact of the normal assumption, and find weak evidence under a Student-t model. [12] Our design should therefore treat the stress correlation (0.7) as a *scenario*, not a certainty.
- **Volatility targeting's Sharpe benefit is asset- and sample-specific.** Strong in Moreira–Muir equity/factor results [6], confirmed for US equities in Harvey et al. [7], but negligible for bonds/FX/commodities; later work finds it does not hold universally. Use it to control risk and drawdown, and treat Sharpe improvement as a bonus.
- **Intraday-vs-swing correlation evidence is thin.** No single primary paper measures the correlation of a value-dip swing sleeve against a specific intraday algo sleeve. The regime evidence ([9][10][11]) plus the FINRA/Alpaca observation that intraday risk can be ~50% higher than daily-close data implies [19] support the *direction*; the *magnitude* (0.7) is a design assumption [INFERENCE].
- **Risk parity's 2022 episode** (stocks and bonds falling together, leveraged bond exposure turning from hedge to liability) is widely reported in the trade press but I did not verify a primary, citable source in this pass; treat as REPORTED background, not a load-bearing number.
- **Kelly with estimated parameters.** The 20:2:1 means/variance/covariance sensitivity [8] is model- and utility-dependent; it is a strong argument for fractional Kelly and small sleeve caps, not a precise calibration.
- **Cost estimates.** The 9.97/11.02 bps market-impact numbers [16] are for institutional equity portfolios; a retail Alpaca algo will face different (often worse, on wide-spread small caps) slippage. Re-measure with own fills before trusting them.
- **Backtest headline risk.** Any sleeve-comparison Sharpe over 2026-01-02→2026-09-11 is a single-regime, ~175-day estimate; the deflated Sharpe framework [25] says do not treat it as evidence of skill.

## Implications for our system

- **Replace the 70/30 *capital* split with a 70/30 *capital* split plus an explicit 75/25 *risk* target.** Persist both numbers in the house risk gate; alert when realised risk shares drift >10 pp from 75/25. (Component: `SleeveAllocator` config block.) [1][derive]
- **Make the house risk gate the only place that sizes positions.** Sleeves emit *intents* (target exposure within their own vol target); the house gate applies vol targeting + CVaR budget + drawdown ladder and returns a scalar scale factor per sleeve. This removes the double-counting path where both sleeves size at full Kelly. [8][13][14]
- **Cap each sleeve at a fractional-Kelly ceiling and cap the *sum*.** Concretely: per-sleeve cap = 0.5 × house-implied Kelly; house aggregate must remain <1.0 × house Kelly; hard-block any configuration whose sum approaches 2× (the growth-zero point). [8]
- **Size on stress correlation, not calm correlation.** The house CVaR/correlation engine must carry a stress ρ (default 0.7) and re-run the worked example above; the "Opportunity Score high / Trade Permission BLOCKED" output must name the binding gate (correlation, CVaR, drawdown ladder, or vol target). [9][10][4]
- **Add a cost-inclusive sleeve gate.** Before any sleeve signal becomes an order, compute expected round-trip cost from realised fills; block the intraday sleeve if projected annual cost drag exceeds 20% of its traded capital. (Component: `CostGate`, feeds `CostModel` from own executions, not vendor assumptions.) [16][17]
- **Implement virtual (not legal) sub-accounting now, real sub-accounts later.** Mirror each sleeve as a virtual ledger row set keyed by `client_order_id` prefix; keep cash and margin reservations recorded per sleeve even though Alpaca pools buying power; migrate to OmniSub sub-accounts when live capital justifies it. [20]
- **Enforce a BLOCK (not net) same-symbol policy.** The overnight holder owns the symbol for the day; the intraday sleeve is barred from it. This preserves per-sleeve attribution, which is a precondition for every scorecard metric below. [derive; 21]
- **Rebalance quarterly with drift bands and an edge hurdle.** Copy the Alpaca Rebalancing semantics (drift_band absolute ±5 pp / relative ±20%, calendar quarterly, cooldown 7 days) and add the extra requirements: ≥60 trading days live, posterior P(better) ≥ 0.75, deflated-Sharpe gate. Never reallocate on a single month's result. [21][25][27][28][29]
- **Adopt the scorecard table above verbatim as the `SleeveScorecard` schema**, and require every sleeve report to carry: Sharpe (with SE), Sortino, Calmar/MAR (marked "unstable <36m"), profit factor, expectancy, payoff ratio, turnover, capacity, utilisation, 1-day 97.5% CVaR (calm and stress), Ulcer Index, tail ratio, Euler risk contribution, and deflated Sharpe. [15][22][23][24][25][4]
- **Wire the MCP/tooling boundary so the execution process is the only credential holder.** The research layer talks to the execution layer over the secure API/MCP surface; the Alpaca MCP server (with `ALPACA_TOOLSETS` scoped and `ALPACA_PAPER_TRADE=true` until live opt-in) is the natural seam, keeping all keys and broker libraries outside the research repo. [30][20]
