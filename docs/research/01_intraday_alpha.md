# Intraday Alpha for US Cash Equities — Setups, Evidence, and Parameter Defaults

**Bottom line (4 lines).**
1. The only intraday setups with credible *academic* support are (a) the Zarattini/Aziz *filtered* 5-minute ORB on high-relative-volume "stocks in play," and (b) the Gao et al. 2018 first-half-hour→last-half-hour momentum effect — and **both are contested**: independent replications put the ORB family at median Sharpe ≈ 0.05–0.20 and the first-half-hour signal at −0.25 [17], and the intraday-momentum predictor dies out-of-sample after 2013 [5].
2. Unfiltered index-ETF ORB (SPY/QQQ/IWM, 15-min range, 2010–2025) shows **no edge**: average post-breakout drift −1.8 bps (upside) / −3.5 bps (downside), indistinguishable from zero and negative before costs [17].
3. The genuinely *robust* intraday structure is the U-shaped volume/volatility seasonality [11][12] plus the **overnight-vs-intraday return split** [13][14][7] — which is exactly why an intraday sleeve and a close-to-close swing sleeve should be near-uncorrelated, and why the overnight drift's post-2021 death [8] is *good* news for sleeve separation but bad news for any "hold overnight" tweak.
4. Treat every headline CAGR below as REPORTED unless labelled PROVEN; design to the *conditional probabilities* (RVOL, gap/ATR, opening-range length, time-of-day cutoffs) and budget **10–25 bps** round-trip execution on the first hour [17][7].

## Findings

### A. Opening Range Breakout (ORB) — the Zarattini/Aziz line

1. **Base paper:** Zarattini & Aziz (2023) test a QQQ 5-minute ORB: if the first 5-min candle (9:30–9:35) closes up, go long at the open of the second candle; if down, go short; stop at the opposite extreme of the first candle; profit target **10R**; flatten at the close if neither is hit. Reported **~1,484% total return on TQQQ vs 169% for passive QQQ (2016–2023)** and **~33% annualized alpha net of commissions** [1][22]. *REPORTED — backtest, leveraged ETF, single-instrument.*
2. **Opening-range length matters enormously and monotonically** in the same paper's "stocks in play" table: **5-min = 1,637% total / 41.6% ann. / Sharpe 2.81 / 12% MDD**; **15-min = 272% / 17.4% / 1.43 / 11%**; **30-min = 21% / 2.3% / 0.21 / 35%**; **60-min = 39% / 4.1% / 0.40 / 21%** (2016–2023) [2][23]. *REPORTED. The 5-min result is the load-bearing one and it depends on the RVOL screen.*
3. **The "stocks in play" filter does the heavy lifting.** Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz (2024) restrict to: price > \$5; 14-day avg volume > 1M; 14-day ATR > \$0.50; **first-5-min relative volume ≥ 100%**; and trade only the **top 20 names by opening RVOL**, risking ~1% of capital with a **stop at 10% of 14-day ATR** and EOD flattening [2]. Reported **1,637% net / Sharpe 2.81 / ~36–41.6% ann. alpha / ~12% max DD, 2016–2023** [2][19][22].
4. **Criticism:** the unfiltered ORB rule is weak; the RVOL "stocks in play" screen is where the return comes from, and an independent replication over 2010–2026 reports **full-sample Sharpe ≈ −0.06 and negative out-of-sample Sharpe** for the broader rule [22][17]. **Survivorship bias** in the eligible universe and **slippage/commission realism** on tight-stop intraday systems are the two most-cited threats [17][19]. *CONTESTED.*
5. **Direct index-ETF replication (SPY/QQQ/IWM, 634 evenly-sampled days 2010–2025, 1-min bars, 720,609 RTH bars):** 15-min ORB: **920 upside breaks → −1.8 bps mean drift 3h later; 923 downside breaks → −3.5 bps**; both inside 2-SE bands all session. Matched control (same direction, same minute, random day): **breakout −0.8 bps vs control +1.2 bps across 7,302 paired events, diff −2.0 bp, p = 0.15**; year-by-year wanders −24 bps (2011) to +17 bps (2025) [17]. *PROVEN for that sample: no measurable raw ORB edge on index ETFs before costs.*
6. **Independent multi-symbol ORB backtest (1,178,668 trades, 614 symbols):** win rates **53.78% (5-min), 50.97% (15-min), 49.38% (30-min)** — note the ordering *reverses* across the vendor's own symbol-level averages (5-min 52.2%, 15-min 52.5%, 30-min 52.9%) and **no commissions or slippage** are modelled [19]. *LEAD, not evidence.*
7. **Academic ORB (futures/currencies, not equities):** Holmberg, Lönnbark & Lundström (2013) find ORB returns "significantly higher than zero" and success rate above a fair game — but "significantly positive average return" ≠ high win rate; it is a trend-following payoff [6]. *PROVEN for that sample; instruments are not US cash equities.*
8. **No-trade-first-N-minutes evidence:** the Alpaca reference implementation deliberately **skips the first 15 minutes** ("the high volatility can lead to poor performance") and only buys between **+15 and +60 minutes after the open** [15]. Independent microstructure evidence: the **first 5 minutes** carries roughly **triple** the day's average volatility in one exchange study, with the **widest quoted spreads of the day** at the open (S&P 500 spreads ~**24 bps immediately after the open**, ~**13 bps by 9:40**) [7]. *PROVEN qualitatively; the "skip" is an execution-cost decision, not a signal.*
9. **The asymmetry of ORB** (gains come from a few 10R winners, most trades small losers) is exactly the payoff profile that **survives few trades per day** and is **highly sensitive to fill quality**; the same literature that inflates ORB on tight stops is the one that says retail day traders net of costs lose [1][9][21]. See §G.

### B. Intraday momentum and the U-shaped seasonality

10. **Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (2018, JFE):** the **first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return** using S&P 500 ETF data **1993–2013**; statistically and economically significant; **stronger on high-volatility days, high-volume days, recession days, and major macro-news days**; appears in ~10 other ETFs; attributed to infrequent rebalancing + late-informed trading [4]. A timing strategy on the first-half-hour sign is widely quoted at **~6.67% ann. ret / 6.19% ann. vol / Sharpe ≈ 1.08** [4, secondary summaries]. *REPORTED for the Sharpe; PROVEN for the in-sample predictability.*
11. **Decay:** Rosa (2022, J. Futures Markets) re-tests the overnight→last-half-hour signal and finds **"the predictability disappears in the out-of-sample period."** A **Markov-switching** model finds **two regimes**, so the edge is state-dependent; an **always-active strategy underperforms a threshold/regime-filtered one**, and calendar-time evaluation alone gives false conclusions [5]. *PROVEN decay for that specification.*
12. **Independent catalogue replication:** across **27 replicated intraday/overnight strategies** (median 36-year window) the **median Sharpe is 0.05 vs 0.37 for the full catalogue**; **44% are negative**; only **19% clear significance vs 48% elsewhere** (rank test p = 1e-5) — "the weakest family in the library." Named: intraday time-series momentum **−0.94**; intraday time-series reversal **−0.91**; gap-fill **−0.34**; first-half-hour momentum **−0.25**; the ORB paper **−0.20**; overnight-drift papers ≈ 0; **market intraday momentum +0.45** (one of the few positives) [17]. *REPORTED catalogue; strongest single piece of decay evidence.*
13. **U-shape seasonality:** volume and volatility are highest at the open and close, lowest midday; the first hour is commonly **~25–35% of daily volume** for active US equities (25–28% on active US futures) [11][12]. **Closing auctions** are **~9–10% of total US equity volume** in 2025 — **NYSE median 12.43% (14.03% notional-weighted), Nasdaq 10.12% median (9.18% notional-weighted)** [20]. *PROVEN stylized fact; the exact first-hour share is venue/sample-dependent.*
14. **Why it matters for signal count:** if ~1/3 of volume and a disproportionate share of the day's volatility occur in the first hour, the number of *tradable* intraday opportunities is heavily front-loaded; conversely, midday breakouts are low-participation and fade more often. This is the empirical basis for the RVOL/time-of-day gates in §D–E.

### C. VWAP-anchored strategies (trend vs range)

15. **VWAP as benchmark vs VWAP as signal are different problems.** The execution literature treats VWAP as a *benchmark* for schedule-matching algos; implementation-shortfall work argues arrival-price benchmarks capture true economic cost better, and VWAP slippage is decomposed into timing/impact/opportunity, not a single number [academic execution literature]. *PROVEN for the benchmark claim; INFERENCE for its use as an intraday entry signal.*
16. **Practitioner regime rule (consensus, not academic):** use **VWAP pullback continuation when price holds one side of a sloping VWAP and pullbacks are shallow**; use **VWAP reversion when price repeatedly crosses a flat VWAP** and extensions reach outer bands [vendor/practitioner consensus]. *REPORTED/LEAD.*
17. **Quantified VWAP/AVWAP claims are vendor-grade and inconsistent:** one guide claims **62–68% win rate, 2:1 RR, "500+ trades, ES/NQ/SPY, 2022–2024"** — I could not verify that study exists; another claims **65–72%** (example backtest 67.7% on NAS100/XAUUSD, not ES/NQ/SPY); an ES AVWAP study with a long sample shows **~29%** on one variant and **~47%** on another; one ES anchored-VWAP study covers **only 11 trades**. [vendor sources] *CONTESTED/UNPROVEN — do not size off these.*
18. **VWAP reversion, SPY/QQQ:** one SPY 5-min backtest shows **54.2% win rate with a gap filter vs 47.8% without**; a plain 15-min SPY/QQQ reversion test lost **−7.7% at 47.4% win rate**; QQQ band-touch (not VWAP) mean reversion claims **61–63% at 2σ and ~71% at 3σ** [vendor]. *LEAD only.*
19. **Trend-day vs range-day classification evidence (heuristic thresholds):** **ADX < 20 = range; ADX > 25 = trend; 20–25 = transitional/gray** is the most commonly cited convention (not a peer-reviewed cutoff) [practitioner]. Better-grounded day-type evidence from the futures literature: **first break of the initial balance is nearly a coin flip (NQ 51.9%, ES 53.1% up-first)**, so "first break direction" is *not* a standalone edge; the usable signal is whether the break **holds/accepts** outside the range [practitioner]. *CONTESTED.*
20. **Anchored VWAP (AVWAP):** the strongest quantitative anchor evidence is anecdotal and instrument-mismatched (see 17); the concept (anchor to a structural pivot/event, trade the first pullback) is reasonable but **has no peer-reviewed track record** in US cash equities. *UNPROVEN.*

### D. Gaps: gap-and-go vs gap-fade

21. **Gap fill probability is strongly decreasing in gap size (relative to ATR).** A large-sample NQ study (2,791 sessions, 2015-01-05→2025-12-30, RTH 9:30–16:00 ET, gap = RTH open − prior RTH close) reports **60.3% full fill by close** overall, and fill probability of **77.8% for tiny gaps (< 0.3× ATR), 42.0% (0.3–0.7×), 25.6% (0.7–1.2×), 8.2% (> 1.2× ATR)**. Partial fills: 25% fill = 86.6%, 50% = 76.1%, 75% = 68.0% [18]. *REPORTED — private, non-peer-reviewed; methodology (ATR lookback, "fill" definition) not fully disclosed.*
22. **US single-stock gaps:** academic work documents a **robust overnight→intraday reversal** (gap-up gives back part of the gain intraday, 2011–2024) and large reversals after extreme gaps, **non-linear in gap size and stronger in illiquid names** (a microcap study of 3,133 gaps ≥50% shows negative average intraday returns) [academic gap-reversal literature]. The classic **Berkman et al. (2012)** mechanism: retail attention inflates the open, then it reverses intraday; roughly **+0.1% overnight followed by ≈ −0.07% intraday** on average, worse in high-attention/illiquid names [14]. *PROVEN directionally; the magnitude is sample-specific.*
23. **The Alpaca Momentum-Trading-Example (primary code, verified line-by-line) implements gap-and-go with these exact thresholds:** universe price **\$2–\$13**, prior-day dollar volume **> \$500k**, **todaysChangePerc ≥ 3.5%** to screen, entry requires **daily_pct_change > 4%**, **price > first-15-min high**, **today's volume > 30,000 shares**, **MACD(12,26) histogram > 0 and rising for 3 bars**, and **MACD(40,60) > 0 and rising**; entry only in the **+15 to +60 minute** window; **stop = last 5-min swing low − \$0.01** (else −5%), **target = entry + 3 × risk**, plus a MACD-exit; **liquidate at market in the final 15 minutes**; risk **0.1% of portfolio per trade** [15]. *PROVEN as the code's actual logic; NOT a validated strategy.*
24. **RVOL interaction:** practitioner consensus is that **high RVOL (≥1.5–2×; gap-and-go often cited ≥3×) shifts the conditional probability toward continuation**, while low/declining RVOL precedes fades; RVOL is a *confirmation* filter, not a standalone trigger, and the academic evidence for RVOL-specific threshold win rates is essentially absent [vendor/practitioner]. *LEAD/REPORTED.*

### E. Relative volume, opening drive, HOD/LOD, first-hour range as day-type tools

25. **RVOL ranking is the single highest-value filter in the ORB line:** Zarattini's edge comes from ranking the day's **top 20 by opening (first-5-min) relative volume** [2]. This is the strongest *empirical* support for an RVOL gate, but it is one paper's backtest [2][22].
26. **First-hour range / initial balance:** the first-hour range is broken on the large majority of sessions in futures studies; the break itself is near-random in direction (see 19), so the **first-hour range is a *volatility/context* tool (is the day compressing? extending?), not a directional signal** [practitioner]. Overnight-range-vs-ATR heuristics: overnight range **< 25% of daily ATR** leans trend-day; **> 60% of daily ATR** leans range day [practitioner]. *CONTESTED heuristics.*
27. **HOD/LOD breakouts:** no credible independent, cost-inclusive evidence places a robust edge on generic HOD/LOD breakouts in US cash equities; they inherit the same ORB-family negative replication bias [17]. *UNPROVEN.*

### F. Mean reversion at the open, short-term reversal, and the overnight/intraday decomposition

28. **Short-horizon reversal exists but is fragile:** about **90% of idiosyncratic price shocks are permanent**, the ~10% temporary component decays with a **half-life ≈ 2.5 days**; reversal strength depends on turnover (low-turnover reverses, high-turnover momentum) [short-term-reversal literature]. *PROVEN at daily horizon; not directly intraday.*
29. **Opening-period reversal is documented but not universal:** high-frequency index-gap studies find statistically significant **intraday reversals at the open** (after roughly the second minute, decaying within ~10 minutes) and a mean-reverting tendency that is **particularly significant ~120 minutes after the open**, while other NYSE studies find **significantly positive first-30-minute returns** (price discovery). So "the first 30 minutes mean-revert" is **not a universal fact** [academic intraday studies]. *CONTESTED.*
30. **The overnight/intraday decomposition is the structural reason the two sleeves can be uncorrelated.** US equities earn most of their return **overnight** (close→open) and much less intraday; e.g. 1998–2020 the 2:00–3:00 am ET hour alone averaged **3.7% p.a. (1.48 bps/day, t = 7.1)** and the full overnight session averaged **3.6% p.a.**, while the 9:00–10:00 ET hour averaged **−1.2 bps** (t = 2.6) [7]. Lou, Polk & Skouras (2019) show **momentum profits accrue overnight while several other anomalies accrue intraday, often with opposite signs**; a one-SD rise in their overnight-minus-intraday spread predicts ≈**+1.0%/month** higher close-to-close returns (~18% of monthly vol) [13]. *PROVEN.*
31. **The overnight drift is now dead post-2021:** the same authors report the 2:00–3:00 window "has averaged close to zero since 2021" while volatility (VIX mean 20.4→19.4) and overnight liquidity share (15%→16%) barely moved; the driver is **compression in end-of-day order-imbalance dispersion (RSV std 6.5% → 2.9%)** [8]. Pre-cost Sharpe of the 2:00–3:00 long was **1.1, falling to −0.5 after bid-ask spreads**; widening to 1:30–3:30 gave pre-cost 1.3 but post-cost **0.3** [7]. *PROVEN.*

### G. Realistic expectations after costs, and decay with crowding

32. **Retail day-trading base rates are brutal:** Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (Taiwan, 1992–2006) find **fewer than 1%** of day traders predictably profitable net of fees (~3% under looser definitions), with costs assumed at **10 bps round-trip commission + 30 bps sales tax** [9]. Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti's Brazilian futures study (2013–2015 entrants) finds **97% of those who persisted > 300 days lost money**, **1.1%** beat the minimum wage, **0.5%** beat a bank teller's salary [21]. *PROVEN for those markets; the US may differ but the direction is unambiguous.*
33. **Post-publication decay:** McLean & Pontiff (2016) find anomaly returns **~26% lower out-of-sample and ~58% lower post-publication**, with the largest decays where trading is expensive (illiquid, high-idiosyncratic-risk, short-sale-constrained names) — i.e. **high-turnover, fast-decaying intraday signals are the most exposed** [10]. *PROVEN on the anomaly panel; INFERENCE for any specific intraday setup.*
34. **Costs you must budget (2025–2026, Alpaca, primary):** commission-free equities, but **sell-side SEC fee \$0.0000278 per dollar** and **FINRA TAF \$0.000166 per share (max \$8.30/order)** [16]. On top, **effective spread ~10–25 bps in the first hour** for US large caps (≈24 bps right after the open, ≈13 bps by 9:40) [7]. A single round-trip on a liquid mega-cap costs roughly **10–15 bps all-in**; on a \$2–\$13 low-float name (the Alpaca universe) it is materially worse. *PROVEN for fee schedule and spread levels; the "all-in" figure is INFERENCE.*
35. **Win rate / payoff expectations after costs (synthesis):** ORB-type breakout systems are **low-win-rate, high-payoff** (≈40–55% wins, winners several × losers); VWAP reversion is **higher-win-rate, small-payoff** (≈45–55% raw, 55–65% only with heavy regime filters); gap-continuation is conditional on RVOL/gap-size; and **every one of these goes negative after 10–25 bps round-trip unless the median trade travels well beyond the spread** [1][6][17][19, 18, vendor]. *MIXED.*

## Contested or unproven

- **Zarattini ORB headline (Sharpe 2.81, 1,637%, 41.6%).** REPORTED backtest on a *filtered* universe; independent replication of the broader rule gives **Sharpe ≈ −0.06**; no live record; survivorship and fill realism unverified. Treat the *filter design* as the learnable part, not the return.
- **VWAP / anchored-VWAP win rates (62–68%, 65–72%, 55–65%).** Vendor/marketing claims; the specific "500+ trades ES/NQ/SPY 2022–2024" study could not be verified; ES AVWAP samples of **11 trades** and conflicting **29% vs 47%** results show the evidence is not a body of research. UNPROVEN.
- **Gao et al. first-half-hour→last-half-hour momentum.** PROVEN in-sample 1993–2013; **dies out-of-sample** [5]; catalogue replication **−0.25** [17] (one variant **+0.45**). Do not treat as a durable edge.
- **Gap-fill percentages (77.8% / 8.2% by ATR bucket).** Single private NQ study, methodology partly undisclosed, **futures not cash equities**; the *direction* (small gaps fill more) is supported by independent samples, the *levels* are not.
- **ADX <20/>25 trend/range cutoffs.** Practitioner convention; the honest evidence is that **first-break direction is ~50/50** and only *acceptance* carries information.
- **First-30-minute mean reversion.** Literature is contradictory: opening reversal documented in some high-frequency samples, **positive first-30-min returns** in others. Not a universal law.
- **Widely repeated but unsourced:** "the first hour is 25–35% of daily volume" (venue/sample-dependent; the close is the better-measured number at ~9–14% [20]); "RVOL ≥ 1.5 improves win rate" (no controlled study found).
- **Cost-inclusive apples-to-apples sleeve comparison 2026-01-02→2026-09-11:** *no public source does this* for these sleeves. Any comparison you produce is original work; the numbers above are the priors to beat, not results to cite.

## Implications for our system

- **Add a first-class `day_type` classifier to the Intraday Algo sleeve**, emitted by 10:00 ET, carrying `{opening_range_5m_pct_of_atr, rvol_first5, adx_14, vwap_slope, gap_pct, gap_atr_bucket}`. Gate: **RVOL ≥ 2.0 AND top-20 rank AND price/ATR filters** before any ORB signal is allowed to fire [2].
- **Make the opening-range length a config, defaulting to 5 minutes for RVOL-filtered names and disabling raw ORB for ETFs entirely** — the SPY/QQQ/IWM replication shows no raw edge and negative costs [17]. Do not ship a 15-min ETF ORB.
- **Hard-code `no_entry_before: 09:35` and `no_entry_after: 11:00` (ET), `flat_by: 15:50`** into the signal contract, and make overnight holding an *impossible* state for the intraday sleeve (reject any order that would survive the close) [15][1].
- **Split gap logic into two explicitly named strategies with opposite signs:** `GAP_GO` (gap ≥ 4% + RVOL ≥ 3× + above 15-min high + rising MACD) and `GAP_FADE` (only when |gap| < 0.3× ATR), and **block GAP_FADE for |gap| > 1.2× ATR** (8.2% fill) [15][18].
- **Cost model must be a gate, not a metric.** Require `expected_move_bps > 3 × round_trip_cost_bps` before emitting a signal; model round trip at **≥12 bps liquid / ≥30 bps low-float** and include SEC/TAF pass-through [16][7]. Most retail-scale ORB setups fail this on \$2–\$13 names.
- **Size each sleeve off its own CVaR, not a fixed 70/30.** Given the intraday family's **median replication Sharpe 0.05 vs 0.37** and **44% negative** [17], the house gate should cap intraday-sleeve capital at the level where sleeve CVaR ≤ swing-sleeve CVaR, and re-balance when either sleeve's realized/max-DD ratio breaches 1.5×.
- **Keep Trade Permission and Opportunity Score strictly orthogonal in the API.** The intraday sleeve's "no BUY" states must be `OPPORTUNITY_LOW` (signal score below threshold) vs `PERMISSION_BLOCKED` with `binding_gate ∈ {sleeve_cvar, house_drawdown, correlation_stress, regime, cost_gate, time_gate}` — never a generic "no trade." The research layer's Opportunity Score should not be allowed to *raise* intraday permission.
- **Instrument the RVOL gate against realized outcomes from day one** (win rate, avg win/loss, trades/day, bps-vs-spread per trade) so the highest-value filter [2] can be validated or falsified on our own data within weeks, instead of trusting a contested backtest.
- **Never ship the overnight/overnight-adjacent variant.** The 2:00–3:00 drift is ≈0 post-2021 with post-cost Sharpe −0.5 to +0.3 [7][8]; keep the swing sleeve as the overnight-return source and let the intraday sleeve be open-to-close only — that is also the cleanest structural source of low sleeve correlation [13][14].
- **Treat every ORB/VWAP headline number in this brief as a prior to beat in the 2026-01-02→2026-09-11 sleeve comparison, not as an input.** Log per-trade `{entry_ts, exit_reason, r_multiple, rvol, adx, gap_pct, spread_bps}` so the comparison is cost-inclusive and decomposes alpha between *signal* and *execution*.
