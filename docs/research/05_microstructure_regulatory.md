# US Equity Market Microstructure, Data Feeds & 2025-2026 Regulatory/Margin Reality

**Bottom line (4 lines).** Nothing in the 2024 Reg NMS "tick size / access fee" package is live for you in September 2026: Rules 612 and 610(c) were stayed, then deferred twice, and now bind no earlier than the first business day of November 2027, while the round-lot and odd-lot definition changes *did* go live (Nov 3 2025 and May 4 2026). The single biggest 2026 change for a day-trading desk is FINRA's retirement of the pattern-day-trader rule: effective **June 4, 2026**, with a firm phase-in ending **October 20, 2027** (not 2026) - the correct replacement to monitor is the new **intraday margin deficit / IML** standard, not the old $25,000 PDT field. For honest intraday research you cannot use a free/retail feed: free tiers are either IEX-only (~2-3% of consolidated volume) or 15-minute-delayed SIP, and the cheapest *real-time-full-SIP* path measured is **$99/mo (Alpaca Algo Trader Plus)**, versus $199/mo for real-time on Massive/Polygon; you need NBBO quote data, not 1-minute bars, to avoid look-ahead on fills. Retail marketable orders *are* typically filled at or better than NBBO - the 2025 JFE evidence puts wholesaler price improvement at ~27% of the quoted spread (~15.8 bps of a 58.35 bps spread) - which is the empirically defensible fill assumption for both sleeves.

## Findings

1. **The 2024 Reg NMS final rule is adopted and effective, but its two headline economic provisions have been deferred to late 2027.** Release No. 34-101070 ("Regulation NMS: Minimum Pricing Increments, Access Fees, and Transparency of Better Priced Orders"), adopted Sept 18 2024, published 89 FR 81620 (Oct 8 2024), effective Dec 9 2024, introduced (a) a **$0.005 minimum pricing increment** for NMS stocks >=$1.00 whose time-weighted average quoted spread is <=$0.015 (otherwise $0.01 remains), (b) **access-fee caps reduced to $0.001/share** for protected quotations in stocks >=$1.00 and **0.1% of quotation price** for stocks <$1.00, (c) a new Rule 610(d) requiring all exchange fees/rebates to be determinable at execution, and (d) accelerated round-lot/odd-lot definitions [1][2].

2. **Implementation chronology of Rules 612 and 610(c): stayed -> Nov 2026 -> Nov 2027.** Petitions for review were filed in the D.C. Circuit; the SEC granted a **partial stay on Dec 12 2024** (Release No. 101899) of Rules 600(b)(89)(i)(F), 610(c) and 612. The D.C. Circuit **denied** the petition on **Oct 14 2025** (*Cboe Global Markets, Inc. v. SEC*, No. 24-1350). On **Oct 31 2025** the SEC extended compliance (**Release No. 104172, 90 FR 51418, Nov 17 2025**) to the **first business day of November 2026**, and on **June 11 2026** (**Release No. 34-105656, 91 FR 36022, June 15 2026**) extended again to the **first business day of November 2027** - explicitly citing crowding with other 2026 market-structure go-lives (23/5 trading, Rule 605, odd-lot tape additions) [2]. **Practical consequence: on 2026-09-12 the $0.01 tick and the pre-2024 access-fee caps still govern; sub-penny quoting of >=$1.00 names is not yet in force.**

3. **The round-lot and odd-lot tape changes were NOT stayed and are live.** The partial stay covered only 600(b)(89)(i)(F)/610(c)/612; Rule 610(d), Rule 603(b), and the round-lot/odd-lot definitions were unaffected [2]. The amended **round-lot definition became operative Nov 3 2025** (tiered 100 / 40 / 10 / 1 shares by average closing price), and the amended **odd-lot information definition (Rule 600(b)(69)) had a compliance date of the first business day of May 2026 (May 4 2026)**, with some SIP dissemination elements deferred further [2]. *The exact step-downs of the round-lot tiers are widely discussed but not fully pinned in the sources captured -> treat tier-effective dates beyond the initial Nov 3 2025 date as UNVERIFIED.*

4. **Consolidated-tape governance actually changed structure, not just fees.** The SEC approved the new **Consolidated Tape (CT) Plan** on **Nov 20 2024** (Release No. 101672, 89 FR 94924, Nov 29 2024), effective that date, replacing the CTA/CQ/UTP plans and centralizing SIP governance [3][4]. The CT Plan's **first fee schedule was filed Dec 11 2025 and approved June 26 2026** (Release No. 34-105778, 91 FR 40047, July 1 2026) [4]. The CT Plan's implementation milestones: proposed fees within 12 months of the effective date (met); **processor/administrator functions no later than 30 months after the effective date or 90 days after fee approval, whichever is later (~ May 2027 or later)**; administrator (DataCT) selected Dec 17 2025 [4]. **No SEC release titled "Competition in the Consolidated Tape" was found - if the owner is referring to a specific 2025 proposal under that name, it is UNVERIFIED as a distinct rulemaking; the substantive vehicles were the CT Plan plus the December 2025 Rule 611/610 roundtable.**

5. **Rule 605 execution-quality reporting goes live Aug 1 2026; Rule 606 quarterly routing disclosure continues.** Rule 605 amendments set **collection beginning Aug 1 2026**, with the first monthly reports (Aug 2026) published **by end of September 2026**; price-improvement statistics relative to the "best available displayed price" are not required until **Nov 1 2026** [2][5]. Rule 606's quarterly order-routing disclosure regime is unchanged [5].

6. **LULD bands are exactly the standard table, and they widen in the close.** The LULD Plan (pilot May 31 2012; permanent **April 11 2019**) sets bands off a **reference price = arithmetic mean of eligible reported transactions over the prior 5 minutes** (first reference price = primary market open or prior close). Percentage parameters: **Tier 1 and Tier 2 <$3.00, 9:30-3:35 ET** -> >$3.00: **5%**; $0.75-$3.00: **20%**; <$0.75: **lesser of $0.15 or 75%**. **Tier 2 >$3.00, 9:30-4:00 ET** -> **10%**. **All bands are doubled in the last 25 minutes (3:35-4:00 ET) for Tier 1 and for Tier 2 below $3.00** [6]. Tier 1 = S&P 500 + Russell 1000 + select ETPs; Tier 2 = all other NMS stocks (rights/warrants excluded). The reference price is refreshed only if a new value is >=1% away [6].

7. **Halt mechanics and duration.** If the NBO (NBB) equals but does not cross the upper (lower) band, the stock enters a **Limit State**; it exits if all limit-state quotations execute/cancel **within 15 seconds**, otherwise the primary listing exchange declares a **5-minute Trading Pause** (extendable a further 5 minutes), after which trading resumes. If paused in the **last 10 minutes** of regular hours, the primary does **not reopen** and instead runs its closing procedures. A **Straddle State** (NBB below / NBO above the band without a Limit State) can trigger a discretionary pause if trading is disorderly [6]. Halt codes to parse: **LUDP** (volatility pause), **LUDS** (straddle), **MWC1/MWC2/MWC3** (market-wide circuit-breaker levels), **IPO1** (IPO issue not yet trading) [7].

8. **IPO/halt-resume behavior differs from LULD.** New-issue and non-IPO regulatory-halt resumptions use a **5-minute "Initial Display Only Period"** before trading terminates the pause, distinct from the LULD limit-state/auction sequence [7]. **Practical risk: an IPO or a first-day LULD pause can strand a marketable position for 5+ minutes with no guarantee the reopening auction prints near your expected price**, and LULD reopen prices can gap through the band.

9. **Reg SHO / SSR trigger and duration.** Under **Rule 201 (short-sale price test)**, if a covered security's price falls **>=10% from the prior day's official closing price** during regular hours, short sales are restricted to prices **above the current national best bid** for the **remainder of that day and the following trading day** (a Friday trigger restricts Friday and Monday); a further 10% decline can re-trigger [8].

10. **Locate, threshold and borrow economics.** Rule 203 requires a **locate** (or reasonable grounds to borrow) before a short sale. A security becomes a **threshold security** after **5 consecutive settlement days** with fails-to-deliver of **>=10,000 shares AND >=0.5% of shares outstanding**; persistent fails require a **close-out after 13 consecutive settlement days** [8][9]. Borrow on **hard-to-borrow (HTB)** names is quoted as an **annualized % of share value charged daily**, commonly single digits to **50%+** on crowded names [9]. **Retail Alpaca accounts can short only the easy-to-borrow (ETB) list on the standard path**; HTB shorting needs an eligible margin account, an approved locate, locates in **round lots of 100**, and locates are **single-use**; ETB carries **$0 locate/borrow fee**, gated at **$2,000 equity** [10]. *(Broker-specific ETB list state for 2025-2026 is owned by brief 06 and should be cross-referenced there.)*

11. **T+1 is live since May 28 2024.** The SEC amended Rule 15c6-1 to shorten the standard settlement cycle to **one business day** (Release No. 34-96930, adopted **Feb 15 2023**, 88 FR 13872, March 6 2023; effective May 5 2023; **compliance May 28 2024**) [11]. The material second-order risk is **funding/FX**: cross-border or non-USD-funded accounts now have far less time to source USD or settle the FX leg, so prefunding or a standing USD buffer is required [11]. For a USD cash-equity desk in a single US account this is mostly a *cash-account* issue, not an FX one.

12. **Cash-account constraints are the practical day-trading blocker if the account is not margined.** In a cash account, buying with unsettled funds and selling before settlement is a **good-faith violation**; **three in 12 months**, or **one free-riding violation** (buying and selling without sufficient settled cash to pay for the purchase), triggers a **90-calendar-day restriction to settled-cash-only trading** [12][13]. A cash account therefore cannot run an intraday algo that turns over before proceeds settle; the desk must be a **margin account**.

13. **FINRA Rule 4210 replaced the PDT framework with an intraday margin standard, effective June 4 2026.** FINRA **Regulatory Notice 26-10**, published **April 20 2026**, states: **Effective Date June 4, 2026; Phase-in Period ends October 20, 2027** [14]. The SEC approved via **Release No. 34-105226** (April 14 2026), 91 FR 20731 (April 17 2026) [15]. The change **eliminates the pattern-day-trader definition (4+ day trades in 5 business days), the $25,000 minimum-equity requirement, and the "day-trading buying power" computation entirely**, and **supplements (does not replace) existing maintenance margin** [15].

14. **What actually replaces PDT: IML and the intraday margin deficit.** New Rule 4210(a)(17)-(19) and (d)(2): **IML (intraday margin level)** = the cash a customer could withdraw while still meeting maintenance margin (or, as a negative number, the cash needed to reach it). An **IML-reducing transaction** = any purchase or sale (including option exercise/assignment) that reduces IML, an option long expiring, or a cash/securities withdrawal. The **intraday margin deficit** = the absolute value of the **largest negative IML** following any IML-reducing transaction during the day [15]. Firms may either **block deficit-creating trades in real time** or compute the deficit **end-of-day** [15].

15. **Deficit cure timing, the 90-day freeze, and the de-minimis safe harbour.** A deficit must be satisfied **"as promptly as possible"** (by net deposits or IML increases sufficient to equal it); it is deemed satisfied if net deposits/IML increases from end-of-day to the end of a subsequent day equal or exceed it, and it **remains outstanding until satisfied or until the close of the 15th business day after it occurs**. If the customer **makes a practice of** failing and does **not satisfy by the close of the 5th business day**, the firm must enforce procedures preventing new short positions or debit balances (other than closing a short) for **90 calendar days** or until satisfied. Deficits **<= the lesser of 5% of account equity or $1,000**, or arising from extraordinary circumstances, do **not** count as "a practice" [15]. **Portfolio-margin accounts under $5M equity must maintain intraday margin substantially similar to end-of-day margin** [15].

16. **Margin-call/close-out timing under the *old* PDT rule (now retired) for contrast.** PDT accounts that failed a *special maintenance* call within 5 business days were limited to cash-available trading for 90 days; funds deposited to meet PDT minimum equity/maintenance could not be withdrawn for **two business days**; an unmet special maintenance call on the 6th business day required a net-capital deduction by the firm [15]. **None of this survives** post-June-4-2026; monitor IML/IMD instead.

17. **Closing-auction cutoffs are exchange-specific and are the hard constraint on any "close into the closing cross" logic.** **NYSE**: Closing Imbalance Period **3:50-4:00 ET**, Closing Auction at **4:00**; MOC/LOC entry cutoff **3:50 ET**, with only imbalance-offsetting orders accepted after, and **cancellation cutoff 3:58 ET** (narrow Rule 7.35B(j)(2)(B) exception) [16]. **Nasdaq**: NOII dissemination **begins 3:50 ET** (every 10s, then every 1s from 3:55), **MOC cutoff 3:55 ET**, **LOC entry until 3:58 ET**, **IO until 4:00 ET**, **cross at 4:00 ET**; MOC/LOC may not be canceled or modified once posted (3:50) [17]. *The precise NYSE MOC/LOC 3:50/3:58 numbers are REPORTED from secondary summaries of Rule 7.35; the Nasdaq numbers are confirmed from Nasdaq's own FAQ PDF.*

18. **Closing auction is a large, growing liquidity pool and the venue for benchmark/index flow.** Nasdaq's own FAQ states the closing auction is **"almost 10% of Nasdaq's average daily volume"** [17]; Nasdaq's 2025 product page states the Closing Cross executed **414M shares/day and ~$33B/day, ~17% of total Nasdaq volume** in Jan-Dec 2025 [18]. US-wide, the closing auction has recently run in the **~7-10% of consolidated volume** range [18]. **Implication: for a swing sleeve holding to the close, the 3:50-4:00 window is both the best liquidity and the most adverse-selection window.**

19. **Payment-for-order-flow is legal and the retail fill is empirically good.** The SEC **withdrew** the proposed **Order Competition Rule** (and proposed Regulation Best Execution) on **June 12 2025** (FR "Withdrawal of Proposed Regulatory Actions," June 17 2025), stating it does not intend to finalize those proposals [19]; PFOF remains permitted subject to best-execution and Rules 605/606 disclosure [19]. The 2025 *Journal of Financial Economics* study "The Retail Execution Quality Landscape" (Dyhrberg, Shkilko, Werner; JFE vol. 168, 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2025.104051) finds **wholesaler price improvement of ~27% of the quoted spread** in the full sample and **~51% of the quoted spread for an average S&P 500 stock**, versus only **~3%** for exchange executions; in that sample the average **quoted spread was 58.35 bps vs an effective spread of 42.59 bps** (~**15.76 bps** improvement) [20]. **Retail marketable orders are, on average, filled at or better than NBBO** - but the benchmark is NBBO/spread, not a counterfactual midpoint, and the result is an *average*, not a guarantee per order [20].

20. **Free/retail market data is not usable for honest intraday research or live signal generation.** Alpaca **Basic (free)** provides **real-time IEX only** for equities, **historical data limited to the latest 15 minutes**, **200 calls/min**, and **30 websocket symbols**; **Algo Trader Plus ($99/mo)** provides **all US exchanges (full SIP)**, **unlimited websocket symbols**, **10,000 calls/min**, and **no historical restriction since 2016** [21]. Because free SIP is 15-minute-delayed and IEX is a small fraction of consolidated volume, a strategy calibrated on Basic data will be blind to ~97%+ of displayed liquidity and cannot see the NBBO [21][22].

21. **Paid data landscape (2026 snapshot).** **Massive (formerly Polygon)**: Starter **$29/mo** and Developer **$79/mo** are **15-minute-delayed**; **Advanced $199/mo** is the tier with **real-time** stock data [22]. **Databento**: usage-based; a representative 2026 mid-universe minute-bar workload benchmarks at **~$125/mo**, flat Standard ~$199/mo [23]. **ThetaData**: retail tiers **Value $40 / Standard $80 / Pro $160 per month** (Standard+ include tick data and OPRA NBBO quotes) [24]. **Tiingo**: paid tiers commonly **$30-50/mo** [22]. *Vendor prices are REPORTED (mix of official pages and 2026 comparisons) and change frequently; confirm at purchase.*

22. **Minimum honest dataset.** To avoid look-ahead on an intraday strategy you need, at minimum, **time-stamped trades and NBBO quotes (bid/ask/size) at sub-second (ideally ms/us) granularity**, not 1-minute bars - because fills, spread cost, and marketable/limit decisions all depend on the prevailing NBBO and queue, which 1-minute OHLC bars erase. 1-minute bars are adequate only for *signal research*, never for *fill/execution-cost modelling*; use quotes+trades (or at least bar-level trade + NBBO-spread) and model slippage against the observed spread plus the ~15.8 bps retail improvement benchmark [20][21][22][23][24].

23. **Wash-sale rules bind across the two sleeves if both are the same taxpayer.** IRC 1091 disallows a loss if substantially identical securities are bought within **30 days before or after** the sale; per IRS guidance the rule reaches purchases in **different accounts controlled by the same taxpayer**, and also purchases by a **spouse** or a controlled corporation; "substantially identical" has **no bright-line test** (facts and circumstances). The disallowed loss is added to the replacement security's basis [25]. **Two sleeves trading the same ticker (one long, one short, or one realizing a loss while the other buys) can create wash sales the brokers will not net-report.**

24. **Section 475(f) mark-to-market election.** A qualifying **trader** may elect mark-to-market: gains/losses become **ordinary** (reported on **Form 4797 Part II**), and the **wash-sale and capital-loss-limitation rules do not apply** (IRC 475(d)(1)) [26]. The election deadline is the **due date (without extensions) of the prior-year return** - for a 2026 election by an individual that was **April 15 2026** (March 15 for calendar-year partnerships/S-corps); a new taxpayer has **2 months and 15 days after the first day of the year**; late elections are generally disallowed. A change from another method requires **Form 3115** under **Rev. Proc. 2025-23 24.01**; revocation requires a notification statement under 24.02 plus Form 3115 [26]. **This is the single highest-leverage tax decision for a two-sleeve day-trading operation and it is already time-barred for tax year 2026 - the next actionable election is for tax year 2027 (file by the unextended due date of the 2026 return).**

25. **The "2026 aggregate cost-basis 1099-B" claim does not check out for equities.** Aggregate 1099-B reporting is permitted only for **regulated futures, foreign currency, and 1256 option contracts**; ordinary equity sales continue to be reported **transaction-by-transaction**, and the equity cost-basis reporting regime dates to **2011** (covered vs non-covered securities), not 2026 [27]. **Marked UNVERIFIED/refuted: there is no new 2026 aggregate equity cost-basis reporting change.** For a 475(f) trader the broker's 1099-B is not the controlling tax computation anyway [26][27].

26. **Record-keeping expectation.** The IRS expects a trader to **identify investment securities vs trading-business securities on the day of acquisition** (e.g., by holding them in a **separate brokerage account**) and to keep detailed records distinguishing them [26]. **This maps directly onto the two-sleeve architecture: sleeve separation should be reflected in *account/ledger* separation, not only in software.**

## Contested or unproven

- **Tick size / access-fee start date is the most volatile fact in this brief.** It moved three times (Nov 3 2025 -> Nov 2026 -> Nov 2027) and could move again under new litigation or further exemptive relief. **Design to $0.01 ticks and legacy access-fee caps as the operating assumption through at least Nov 2027, with a feature flag.**
- **"Competition in the Consolidated Tape" as a named 2025 rulemaking is UNVERIFIED.** The verifiable instruments are the CT Plan (approved Nov 2024), its fee schedule (approved June 2026), and the December 2025 Rule 611/610 roundtable. If the owner read about a distinct "competition" proposal, it is likely a *fee-schedule* or *roundtable* discussion, not a final rule.
- **The task's "transition period through 2026-10-20" is contradicted by the primary source**, which says the phase-in ends **October 20, 2027**. Verify before encoding a date in config.
- **Retail price-improvement figures are sample- and benchmark-dependent.** The JFE result is NBBO-relative and average across order types; it does not guarantee improvement on a specific odd-lot order, and NBBO-based benchmarking can under/overstate benefit when midpoint or odd-lot liquidity exists outside the NBBO [20].
- **Closing-auction share numbers are inconsistent by definition** ("auction print" vs "on-close flow" vs "Nasdaq-listed only"). 17% is Nasdaq's own figure for *its* volume; US-wide 7-10%. Do not use one where the other is meant [17][18].
- **NYSE MOC/LOC 3:50 and cancellation 3:58 are REPORTED** from secondary summaries of Rule 7.35; Nasdaq's 3:55/3:58 are confirmed from Nasdaq's own FAQ. Do not assume NYSE and Nasdaq are symmetric - they are not.
- **Vendor prices are REPORTED** (mixed official-page + third-party-comparison evidence, some details not machine-readable). Confirm at purchase; tiers change and promotions are common [21][22][23][24].
- **IEX's ~2-3% of consolidated volume** is widely repeated (including in Free/retail-feed discussions) but was not pinned to a single 2025-2026 primary in this pass -> **treat as REPORTED**, directionally safe, not audited.
- **Round-lot tier step-down schedule beyond the initial Nov 3 2025 operative date is UNVERIFIED**; the 100/40/10/1 tiers are confirmed but the phase timing of each step is not.
- **The 2025 JFE study's exact sample period and cost assumptions were not extracted here** - the JFE/SSRN record confirms the paper (DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2025.104051) and the 27%/51% figures, but the headline should be quoted with "their sample" attached, never as a universal constant [20].

## Implications for our system

- **The house risk gate must name the binding constraint, not just emit a boolean.** Add a `trade_permission` object with `{"decision": "ALLOW|BLOCK", "binding_gate": "<cvar|drawdown|correlation_stress|vol_regime|knife_guard|position_size|house_exposure>", "opportunity_score": <0-100>}` so the desk can distinguish "no BUY because valuation is unattractive" (low Opportunity Score) from "no BUY because portfolio risk forbids it" (high Opportunity Score + `BLOCK` + named gate). This is a UI/contract change to `research_decision.json` consumers and to `signald`.
- **Fork the capital ledger before the risk gate, not after.** Reserve explicit sleeves (`value_dip_swing` = 70%, `intraday_algo` = 30%) with a hard per-sleeve cash/BP ceiling so the two never draw on the same buying power; the house gate above both should only *reduce* the aggregate, never let one sleeve raid the other's allocation.
- **Wire the FINRA IML/IMD model into `signald`, not the old PDT model.** Track `iml`, `intraday_margin_deficit` (max negative IML per day), the 5-business-day cure clock, the 15-business-day expiry, and the 90-day freeze state. Because the firm may block in real time *or* compute EOD, the daemon must **enforce the stricter path locally**: pre-trade check on every IML-reducing transaction with the de-minimis safe harbour (<= lesser of 5% equity / $1,000) [15].
- **Add a market-state pre-trade guard using LULD + halt state.** Block new entries when a symbol is in a Limit/Straddle State or a Trading Pause (codes LUDP/LUDS/MWC*/IPO1), and forbid opening positions in the last 10 minutes on a paused name (no reopen). Widening bands at 3:35 ET (doubling) should loosen *stop* thresholds, not tighten them [6][7].
