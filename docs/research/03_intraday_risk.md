# Dividend — Intraday Risk Management for High-Turnover Equity Strategies

**Bottom line (4 lines):**
1. Volatility targeting is the single highest-evidence risk lever for long equity risk: Harvey et al. lift the US-equity Sharpe from 0.40 to 0.48–0.51 and cut vol-of-vol from 4.6% to 1.8%, but the benefit is asset-specific (equities/credit only) and is already almost fully captured by an EWMA daily estimate. [1]
2. For intraday books the binding constraints are not volatility but tail/event risk: daily VaR understates intraday gap and LULD-halt re-open risk, so the house gate should budget on 97.5% ES/CVaR (the Basel FRTB capital measure) with a hard daily-loss kill switch, not on VaR. [7][8][11][29]
3. Full Kelly is unusable for day-trading edges: fractional (half/quarter) Kelly is the operational standard because growth is quadratic near the optimum — half-Kelly retains ~75% of growth at ~half the volatility — and because the underlying edge estimates for day traders are near zero or negative in every large study. [17][18][20][21]
4. Two sleeves (Value-Dip + Intraday Algo) should share one house risk gate but hold separate risk budgets, with capital split by risk not notional (70/30 capital is not 70/30 risk), and a de-risking ladder + kill switch that is path-independent (based on drawdown/ES, not on losing streaks — see the Triple Penance warning). [7][16]

## Findings

1. **Volatility targeting improves risk-adjusted returns for risk assets, not for all assets.** Over US equities 1927–2017 at a constant 10% annualised target, the gross Sharpe rises from 0.40 (unscaled) to 0.48–0.51 (scaled) and is insensitive to the volatility-estimator choice; the improvement is statistically significant (intercept 0.64 bp/day, t = 3.05, Newey–West 30 lags, R² = 0.73). Bond, currency and commodity Sharpe ratios are essentially unchanged, and for the US value factor volatility targeting lowers the Sharpe. [1]
2. **The tail benefit is the robust part, and it applies across 60+ assets:** vol-of-vol falls 4.6% → 1.8% for US equities, left-tail mean shortfall falls materially, and maximum drawdown is lower — this holds even where the Sharpe does not improve (e.g. 10-year Treasury futures, 1988–2017: vol-of-vol 2.5% → 1.2%). [1]
3. **The "leverage ratchet" is real and is the hidden risk of vol targeting.** In Moreira–Muir's baseline (inverse previous-month realised variance), the managed-market weight percentiles are median ≈ 1.0, 75th = 1.6, 90th = 2.6, 99th = 6.4. The ratchet means the strategy levers up most exactly when volatility is lowest — i.e. right before a vol spike. Capping weights at 1.0 or 1.5 does not change the Sharpe but does reduce the alpha. Scaling by volatility (not variance) shrinks the 99th-percentile weight to ~3 and keeps the Sharpe. [2]
4. **Cost of vol-target rebalancing.** Harvey et al. model equity trading at 1.0 bp of notional traded; turnover (annual roundtrips of mean exposure) is ~5×/yr for the most responsive estimate (10-day half-life) to <1×/yr for the slowest. A worked example: a 10-day half-life, 10%-vol strategy costs ≈ 0.066% per year (2 × 1 bp × 71% × 4.66). Because these strategies are near-linear in turnover, intraday rebalancing at, say, 13 rebalances/day (every 30 min) multiplies the cost by ~13–65× relative to a daily rule — i.e. 17 bp–4.3% per year of pure leverage-rebalancing drag at 1 bp/notional [INFERENCE, arithmetic on [1]]. The implication: use a threshold/band update, not continuous intraday re-targeting.
5. **Higher-frequency vol estimates help only marginally for risk assets.** Using 5-minute S&P 500 futures returns (09:15–14:00 CT, liquid window), aggregated to daily realised variance and EWMA-smoothed, "slightly improves" Sharpe, vol-of-vol and left tail versus daily-data estimates; they note they "only scratched the surface" of intraday estimation. They use an EWMA of squared (zero-mean) returns and require ≥270 trading days of history before scaling. [1]
6. **Range/OHLC volatility estimators dominate close-to-close on efficiency.** Relative efficiency vs close-to-close (same sample): Parkinson ~5× (uses High–Low, ignores gaps/drift), Garman–Klass ~7.4× (Open–High–Low–Close, assumes no drift/gaps), Yang–Zhang ~14× (adds overnight & drift, gap-robust). These are theoretical variance ratios from the original papers and are widely reproduced; the RATIOS are [REPORTED] from the primary estimators, not independent replication. [3][4][5]
7. **For 1-minute intraday estimation, microstructure noise is the binding problem, not sample size.** Aït-Sahalia–Mykland–Zhang show that realised variance from ultra-high-frequency returns is biased upward by noise and derive an optimal sparse sampling frequency (order of minutes) that trades off discretisation bias vs noise variance. CRITICAL: 1-min bars are on the noisy side of that optimum for many names; 5-min is the safe default and is exactly what Harvey et al. use. [6][1]
8. **ES is the coherent tail measure and the regulatory capital standard; VaR is not.** Acerbi–Tasche establish ES as coherent (sub-additive, monotone, translation-invariant), fixing VaR's failure in the tail. Rockafellar–Uryasev give the convex-optimisation representation (CVaR as a linear program in the loss-tail), which is how a CVaR budget can be enforced as a constraint rather than an after-the-fact report. [9][10]
9. **FRTB replaced 99% VaR with 97.5% ES for internal-model market-risk capital, while keeping VaR backtests.** Desks are validated by backtesting 1-day VaR at 97.5% and 99% over the most recent 12 months of equally weighted data, plus P&L-attribution tests; the capital metric is 97.5% ES. The Basel QIS (Dec-2017 data, 37 banks) estimated market-risk capital +22% vs Basel 2.5 and market RWAs rising to ~5% of total RWAs. [7][8]
10. **ES is far harder to estimate in small samples than VaR, and this is a first-order risk for an intraday book.** Kondor and Varga-Haszonits–Kondor show ES estimation error is large and can diverge/undergo a "phase transition" (optimisation infeasible, error exponent diverging) precisely where a desk has the least data; ES needs materially larger samples than VaR for equivalent relative precision. With 250 days and a 2.5% tail you have ~6 tail observations per year — statistically meaningless for a direct historical ES. [11][12]
11. **ES backtesting is feasible but is done through proxies.** Moldenhauer–Pitera build a traffic-light-style ES backtest (secured-position statistic), and Hué–Hurlin–Lu (2024) give a duration-severity test that separates frequency and severity of VaR violations — relevant because most intraday "misses" are severity (gap/limit) rather than frequency. Basel itself backtests VaR, not ES, precisely because ES is hard to backtest directly. [13][14][7]
12. **Stop-loss rules only add value under momentum, per Kaminski–Lo.** Their AR(1) criterion: a stop stops losses only when the autoregressive coefficient ρ ≥ the strategy's Sharpe ratio (their "stopping premium" > 0). Under a random walk, a stop lowers expected return. So the evidence does not support "tighter stops improve expectancy" as a universal rule; it supports "tight stops bound loss size but raise stop-out frequency, and only help if the underlying return process is positively autocorrelated." [15]
13. **ATR-based stop calibration (practitioner evidence, labelled REPORTED):** sub-1.0× ATR stops are "very tight," 1.5×–2.0× ATR is the range most cited to reduce noise-induced stop-outs, ≥2.0× ATR reduces whipsaw further but increases average loss. This is not peer-reviewed; treat as [REPORTED practitioner] and calibrate to your own Maximum Adverse Excursion distribution. [INFERENCE]
14. **Stop orders are triggered market orders, and clustered stops create cascades.** The Flash Crash analysis shows aggressive selling (the Waddell & Reed E-mini order) interacting with HFT liquidity withdrawal drove the cascade; resting stops are invisible but, once triggered, become predictable market flow. Conversely, limit orders are the "free option" — Foucault–Kadan–Kandel model the limit-order book as a market where the resting trader effectively grants the counterparty an option, compensated by the spread (adverse selection). The asymmetric implication: use stop-limit or conditional logic, never naked stop-market, and never place stops at round-number/cluster levels. [26][27]
15. **Drawdown recovery is governed by the Triple Penance rule.** Bailey–López de Prado show that under IID returns the expected time to recover a maximum drawdown is ≈3× the time it took to build it, independent of Sharpe (this justifies less strict stop-out rules for higher-Sharpe managers). Under serial correlation the maximum drawdown can be ~65% larger than the IID estimate while the penance is ~17% lower on average — i.e. ignoring autocorrelation understates depth and mis-times recovery. [16]
16. **The "circuit breaker on a losing streak" is dangerous and largely unsupported.** Streak-based halting is practitioner guidance (commonly 2–3 losses → reduce size, 3–5 → stop for the day), but losing streaks are expected under any fixed edge (P(n losses in a row) = (1−p)^n), and cutting sample size after a random streak removes the best recovery trades, converting a bad day into a bad month. The defensible rule is path-independent de-risking (drawdown/ES/trailing risk triggers), not streak counting. [19][16][INFERENCE]
17. **Full Kelly is unusable; fractional Kelly is standard.** Kelly's binary form is f* = p/l − q/g. Near the optimum the growth rate is quadratic, so a λ-Kelly bet earns ≈ λ(2−λ) of full-Kelly growth: half-Kelly ≈ 75% of growth at ~half the volatility, quarter-Kelly ≈ 44% of growth at ~quarter the volatility (variance scales ~λ²). MacLean–Thorp–Ziemba formalise this and the estimation-error penalty: the optimal fraction falls as input uncertainty rises. [17][18]
18. **Real day-trading edge profiles are close to zero or negative**, so Kelly inputs must be shrunk hard. Barber–Lee–Liu–Odean (Taiwan) find the cross-section of day-trader skill is heavily skewed: the vast majority of day traders are unprofitable and only a very small fraction (<1%) earn reliably positive abnormal returns. [20]
19. **The strongest "typical edge" evidence is Brazilian futures:** Chague–De-Losso–Giovannetti report that of day traders who persisted >300 days, 97% lost money and only 1.1% earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage (the base rate you must beat). Treat any backtested day-trading edge with extreme scepticism; require out-of-sample, cost-inclusive, post-2020 evidence. [21]
20. **Risk of ruin has a closed form for fixed-fractional sizing.** With per-trade edge A = p·R − (1−p) (R = avg win / avg loss, loss normalised to 1) and N = capital ÷ risk-per-trade (in risk units), RoR ≈ ((1−A)/(1+A))^N; at R = 1 this reduces to the gambler's-ruin form ((1−p)/p)^N. This gives the first-principles answer to "how many risk units must a sleeve hold." [19]
21. **Correlations rise in crashes but do not literally go to 1.** Longin–Solnik (weekly cross-market correlations 0.23 pre-1987 → 0.39 post-1987) and Ang–Chen (asymmetric correlations: equity portfolio correlations are materially higher in down markets) both support regime-dependent, downside-elevated correlation. FAJ explicitly calls "all correlations go to 1" an exaggeration; ECB work finds equity–bond correlation can fall in turmoil. For a same-account intraday book, the practical rule is to stress-test with correlation → ~0.8–0.9 among long equity names, not 1.0, and to apply a single-factor market beta cap. [22][23]
22. **Intraday volatility has a strongly non-stationary U-shape and jumps on macro announcements.** Andersen–Bollerslev document intraday activity patterns (open/close concentration) and characteristic volatility responses to scheduled macroeconomic announcements. The day structure is itself subject to structural breaks, so a fixed intraday vol curve should be re-estimated (Kokoszka et al. 2024 give change-point tests for the intraday pattern). Implication: a single flat intraday vol target mis-sizes the open and close. [24][31]
23. **Overnight risk is a distinct, sentiment-driven component.** Aboody et al. show overnight returns load heavily on firm-specific sentiment (overnight vs intraday return decomposition), and the practical consequence is that a stop cannot protect a gap: the loss is realised at the re-open, not the stop price. Flattening before the close removes the gap component entirely; if holding overnight, size for the gap distribution, not the intraday stop. [25]
24. **LULD halt/re-open mechanics create a specific intraday risk.** The LULD Plan sets price bands at ±5% (Tier 1, >$3, 09:30–15:35) and ±10% (Tier 2), doubled in the last 25 minutes; a limit state persisting 15 seconds triggers a 5-minute Trading Pause (extendable), with a collared re-opening auction (initial collar 5% wider than the triggering band). If a halt occurs in the last 10 minutes, the primary exchange does not re-open and uses closing procedures. Straddle states may also trigger pauses. [29]
25. **Margin/buying power is a hard sizing cap the risk model must respect.** Reg T initial margin 50%; FINRA minimum maintenance 25% (Alpaca uses 30% for longs >$6, 50% for $2.50–$6, 100% for <$2.50; 50% concentration margin if one security is ≥70% of equity and margin balance ≥$100k). Alpaca grants 2× overnight and up to 4× intraday buying power; unconsumed intraday leverage is interest-free only if flattened intraday. FINRA's 2026 intraday-margin regime (Rule 4210) replaced the PDT rule. [30][28]

## Contested or unproven

- **Vol targeting generalises to intraday/high-frequency?** The Harvey evidence is on daily/monthly sampling; the intraday-data result is "slight" and explicitly exploratory. Extrapolating the 0.40→0.51 Sharpe improvement to an intraday sleeve is CONTESTED / unproven. [1]
- **Range-estimator efficiency ratios (5×, 7.4×, 14×) are theoretical** (variance ratios under idealised price processes) and can be badly violated with gaps, jumps and microstructure noise. Treat as [REPORTED], verified mainly against the original papers. [3][4][5][6]
- **"Tighter stops improve expectancy" is NOT supported.** The directional evidence is the opposite under random walk; Kaminski–Lo's "stop stops losses" requires ρ ≥ Sharpe. Vendor blogs asserting tighter stops improve Sharpe are SEO/practitioner claims, not evidence. [15]
- **Daily-loss-limit / circuit-breaker efficacy is unproven.** No credible randomised or large-sample study shows a daily −X% stop raises Sharpe; the mechanism is a sample-reduction trade-off. The "improves Sharpe and Calmar" claim circulating in practitioner content is unsourced. [REPORTED practitioner]
- **"All correlations go to 1 in a crash" is exaggerated** (FAJ); the defensible statement is equity correlations rise, often sharply, in down markets, and equity–bond behaviour differs. [22][23]
- **Day-trading base rates differ by market.** Barber et al. (Taiwan) show a persistent skilled minority; Chague et al. (Brazil) show ~0% durable edge. US cash equities are between these and closer to "no edge for the average participant." Any claimed intraday edge must be demonstrated, not assumed. [20][21]
- **ES backtests are model-checking proxies, not truth.** Acerbi–Székely-style and duration-severity tests have low power in the small samples an intraday desk actually has. Passing an ES backtest is weak evidence. [13][14][11]
- **The 6.4× tail leverage weight** is a mathematical property of inverse-variance scaling, not a recommendation; whether to cap it is a capital-efficiency choice, not settled science ([2] shows caps preserve Sharpe but cut alpha).

## Implications for our system

1. **Insert a VolTargeter component in front of both sleeves' sizers.** Every order size passes through size = base_risk_budget / (EWMA_vol_20d_half_life × daily_scaling), with a hard cap at 1.5× sleeve NAV and a 270-day warm-up gate. This is the highest-evidence change and directly implements [1].
2. **Adopt 97.5% ES/CVaR as the house tail metric and expose it as a first-class field** in the mandate gate (the same object that already names the binding gate). Replace any VaR-based budget with cvar_975_1d. This aligns the execution layer with FRTB and fixes the VaR-understates-intraday-tail problem [7][8][11].
3. **Make the house risk gate output an explicit two-sided verdict**, satisfying requirement (d): opportunity_score (valuation/alpha) AND trade_permission ∈ {ALLOW, BLOCK(reason)} where reason names the binding gate — e.g. BLOCK(CVaR_BUDGET), BLOCK(DAILY_LOSS_LIMIT), BLOCK(VOL_TARGET_CAP), BLOCK(CORRELATION_CLUSTER). "No BUY because unattractive" must be reported as low opportunity_score, never as a risk block.
4. **Give each sleeve its own RiskBudget object** (per-sleeve per-trade risk, daily loss limit, ES budget, max positions) and a shared HouseGate that sees the sum of gross exposure, net beta, and cluster correlation. The 70/30 capital split must be reflected in RiskBudget as an explicit risk share (target ~50/50 risk), with a documented re-balance rule when realised sleeve vols diverge.
5. **Implement a path-independent DeRiskLadder with a KillSwitch.** Triggers on drawdown/ES/trailing-loss only — never on losing streaks. On breach: cancel resting orders, flatten intraday sleeve, lock new entries until (a) human review and (b) realised vol back inside target. Document Triple-Penance recovery expectation (≈3× drawdown-building time) so re-entry discipline is explicit [16].
6. **Replace naked stops with stop-limit/collared exits and add a pre-trade "cluster proximity" check.** Reject stops within a band of round numbers / prior-day high-low / obvious LULD-band levels. Track Maximum Adverse Excursion per setup and set stop distance to the greater of 1.5× ATR and the 80th-percentile MAE [26][27][15].
7. **Add an explicit LULD/halt state machine.** The risk engine must know if a holding is in Limit/Straddle/Pause state, mark it at the band edge (not last trade), block new entries within ±5 min of a limit state, and force flatten-if-halted in the last 10 minutes (no re-open) [29].
8. **Enforce a hard "flatten intraday sleeve at 15:55 ET" job and a gap-aware sizing model for any overnight carry** in the value-dip sleeve. Overnight size ≤ 50% of intraday-equivalent and priced off a gap (ES) distribution, never off the intraday stop [25].
9. **Wire buying-power/heat accounting into the sizer.** Maintain reserved_buying_power ≥ 25% of Alpaca 4× intraday BP, cap single-name at 10% notional and 25% of 20-day ADV, and pre-empt the 50% concentration margin (trigger at 70% single-name, ≥$100k margin) [30].
10. **Treat every day-trader edge input as shrunk:** require out-of-sample, cost-inclusive (≥5 bp round-trip + slippage), post-2020 validation before an intraday sleeve edge is used to size, and default the Kelly fraction to 0.25–0.5 with an explicit estimation-error haircut [17][20][21].
11. **Estimate intraday vol with a time-of-day-aware model**, not one flat daily number: fit an intraday vol curve (U-shape), re-estimate on a rolling window with a change-point guard, and scale positions down in the first/last 30 minutes and around scheduled macro releases [24][31].
